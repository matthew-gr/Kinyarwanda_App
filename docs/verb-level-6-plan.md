# Verb Builder level 6: advanced (plan)

The product spec gives level 6 one line: "Subjunctive and ongoing actions, then a closing session on helper verbs: ndaza gukina, ngiye gukina, namaze gukina." This plan turns that into lessons. Nothing here is built yet, and every form is an AI draft until a native speaker checks it.

## Where levels 1 to 5 leave a learner

After level 5, a learner can build present, future, "and then", earlier today and before today, with a person as object, and with a family extension (gukinana, gukorera, gukoresha). Every word is still one statement of fact. Level 6 adds the three things a learner needs next to talk with people: asking and suggesting ("let's play"), saying what is going on right now ("I'm playing"), and the helper verbs that frame an action ("I'm going to play", "I've finished playing").

## Three lessons, in this order

Level 6 runs like level 4: one opening screen and its own worked examples for each lesson, with the next lesson straight after the last.

### Lesson 1: wishes and "let's" (the subjunctive)

- **Pattern:** who + root + e, with no time marker. Examples: dukine (let's play), ukine (you should play, or play!), akine (let him or her play), bakine, nkine (may I play?), mukine.
- **Why first:** it is short, very common in speech ("Tugende!", let's go) and needs only one new idea: the ending e instead of a.
- **Tiles:** when gets a "wish, let's" tile with no piece (picture: 🙏), and the ending gets e beside a and the past endings. Vowel stems drop nothing (twicare, let's sit down; twiruke, let's run).
- **Forms:** 6 persons for each of the 19 verbs, about 114 words. Families can come too (dukinane, let's play together), but only once the plain forms are checked.

### Lesson 2: ongoing actions

- **Pattern (to confirm with a native speaker):** a form of -ri ("to be") with the infinitive, as in ndi gukina (I'm playing right now), uri gukina, ari gukina, turi gukina, muri gukina, bari gukina. Many speakers say ndimo gukina instead, and some regions prefer one of the two. The plan teaches whichever the reviewer picks, and the other as a note.
- **Also:** "still" with -racya-: aracyakina (he or she is still playing). It fits here because it also describes an action in progress.
- **Tiles:** the builder makes two words for the first time: a helper tile (ndi, uri, ari …) and the infinitive (gukina). The helper's who has to match, so the who tile still decides the first word.
- **Forms:** 6 persons × 19 verbs for ongoing, plus 6 × 19 for "still": about 228 words.

### Lesson 3: helper verbs (the spec's closing session)

- **The three from the spec:**
  - ndaza gukina: I'm about to play, I'll play shortly (from kuza, to come).
  - ngiye gukina: I'm going to play, I'm off to play (from kugenda, to go).
  - namaze gukina: I've finished playing, I've already played (from kumara, to finish).
- **Three more worth adding:** nshaka gukina (I want to play), nshobora gukina (I can play), ngomba gukina (I must play). They're among the most used verbs in everyday talk and follow the same two-word build. Your call whether level 6 includes them or they wait.
- **Tiles:** a helper tile with its picture (⏩ about to, 🚶 going to, ✅ finished, 💭 want, 💪 can, ❗ must), then the infinitive. The helper carries who and time, so who and when move onto the helper.
- **Forms:** 3 helpers × 6 persons × 19 verbs is about 342 words; with the three extra helpers about 684.

## What changes in the app

- **Engine:** a form can be two words (a helper and an infinitive). The builder grades the helper and the verb as two slots, so the existing come-back and mastery rules still work. The ending slot gains e.
- **Stories:** the story prompt already lists the forms a learner knows by level; level 6 adds "the subjunctive, ongoing actions and helper verbs", so stories can use "let's" and "is playing".
- **Learn and Review:** like the past at level 4, these forms appear only in Verb Builder for now. Showing them on course cards needs the forms drafted for all ~350 course verbs, which is a separate content job.
- **Audio:** about 700 to 1,000 new words, so roughly 50 to 70 Pindo requests, about $6 to $8.
- **Placement:** stays at levels 1 to 3 until the later levels have been checked.

## Left out on purpose

- **Negatives** (sinkina, I don't play; ntabwo nakinnye, I didn't play). They change the front of the word in several ways and deserve their own level: a level 7, or part of a later "questions and negatives" module.
- **Object plus subjunctive** (mumfashe, help me!). Useful, but it combines levels 3 and 6; it can follow once the plain subjunctive is checked.
- **Relative forms** (umwana ukina, the child who plays). These belong with noun classes rather than with verbs.

## Questions for you and the native reviewer

1. Ongoing: ndi gukina, ndimo gukina, or both?
2. Does ndaza gukina mean "I'm about to play" or "I'll play later today", or both, depending on context?
3. Should level 6 include want, can and must, or only the spec's three helpers?
4. Is negation worth a level 7 in Verb Builder, or should it live elsewhere?

## Effort

About the same as levels 4 and 5 together. The new piece of work is the two-word build; the rest reuses the tiles, mastery and opening screens.
